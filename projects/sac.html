"Sắc" E-Commerce

"From requirements to reality — shipped it."

Full-Stack E-Commerce for Vietnamese Traditional Fashion (Áo Dài & Việt Phục)

UX Research Full-Stack

Sắc is a full-stack e-commerce website for a premium Vietnamese traditional clothing brand — Áo Dài and Việt Phục — targeting the accessible luxury segment (3–10 million VND price range) for customers aged 25–45 in major Vietnamese cities.

Built with HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Angular, and MongoDB (Atlas + Compass), the project covered complete system analysis, a full customer-facing interface, and an admin panel — all designed, documented, and developed by a group of 5 within the academic year.

What This Project Was About

The accessible luxury market for traditional Vietnamese fashion is real — people want quality Áo Dài and Việt Phục they can buy online without either paying boutique prices or sacrificing the experience of shopping somewhere that actually understands the product. Sắc was built to fill that gap.

The market gap we responded to

Traditional fashion brands in Vietnam either operate fully offline (boutiques, custom tailors) or list on general platforms like Shopee with no dedicated brand experience. Neither option serves customers who want a reliable, culturally authentic online shopping experience with full e-commerce functionality.

The project scope was comprehensive: product catalog, search, cart, checkout with QR payment, wishlist, blog, customer support chat, email verification, and a full admin panel. This wasn't a prototype — it was a working website designed for an actual brand.

Research & Requirements

  • Market analysis — mapped the competitive landscape for traditional Vietnamese fashion online, identifying why generic platforms don't serve this segment well
  • Target customer — adults aged 25–45 in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, buying for formal occasions, gifting, or cultural events, in the 3–10 million VND price range
  • Requirements gathering — translated the product brief into a structured feature list, separating MVP requirements from enhancements
  • User flow mapping — documented key journeys: registration, browsing, product detail, cart, checkout, order tracking, wishlist, and blog reading
  • Competitor analysis — evaluated existing fashion platforms to understand UX conventions worth keeping and gaps worth filling
Why the price point mattered for design

The 3–10 million VND segment expects brand presentation that matches the price. Low-quality photography, cluttered layouts, and generic templates would undercut the perceived value of the product. Every design decision — typography, product card layout, checkout flow — was weighed against that expectation.

System Design

Before writing any code, the team produced a complete system analysis documenting how the website would actually work — from process flows to database schema.

Diagrams and models:

  • Use case diagrams — guest, customer, and admin actor flows with full interaction coverage
  • BPMN process flows — registration, cart and checkout, and order management workflows
  • Level 0 and Level 1 DFDs — system-level and detailed data flow for each major module
  • MongoDB schema — entity identification, relationships, and constraints for users, products, orders, wishlist, blog posts, and customer support sessions
Key technical decisions

Angular for the frontend (component-based architecture suited the product catalog and admin panel structure), MongoDB Atlas for cloud-hosted storage with MongoDB Compass for local development and schema inspection, GitHub for version control and team collaboration, Draw.io for all system diagrams.

The QR code payment checkout was a deliberate choice: it matches how Vietnamese consumers actually pay for premium purchases offline (bank transfer QR), reducing the cognitive gap between the online and in-store experience.

What the Team Delivered

Customer-facing interface:

  • Registration and login with email verification and password recovery
  • Homepage with hero banner and product collections
  • Full product browsing, search, and category filtering
  • Product detail pages with size guide and product imagery
  • Wishlist, shopping cart, and QR code payment checkout
  • Blog section, About Us page, and customer support chat

Admin panel:

  • User management, product management (add/edit/delete catalog items)
  • Order processing and status management
  • Blog publishing and feedback management

Documentation:

  • Full system analysis — use cases, BPMN flows, DFDs, MongoDB schema
  • Figma designs covering all key screens before development started

My Contribution

Areas I was most hands-on with:

  • Requirements analysis — translating the product brief into a structured, prioritized feature list the team could actually build from
  • System diagrams — use case diagrams, DFDs, and BPMN flows in Draw.io
  • MongoDB schema design — entity modeling, relationship definition, and constraint documentation
  • Frontend development — Angular components for core customer-facing flows
  • Figma screens — design contributions for the customer interface across key journeys

What I Took Away

"Building the whole thing — analysis, design, code, docs — gives you a different kind of understanding than working on just one piece."

The biggest thing this project reinforced was the value of doing the system design before writing code. The BPMN flows and DFDs felt like overhead at first — but when the team got to development, those diagrams answered questions before they became arguments. Disagreements about "how should checkout work?" dissolved when we could point to a process flow we'd already agreed on.

Working across the full stack also helped me understand where the friction lives between design and development. A Figma screen that looks clean can create 10 hours of dev work if the design didn't account for state management, error handling, or edge cases. Going through that myself made me a more realistic designer on future projects.
