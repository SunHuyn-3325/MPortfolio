TiredCity App

"Culture meets code — built for Gen Z and Gen Alpha."

Android E-Commerce App with AI Mệnh Styling & Fashion Chatbot

UX Research Dev

TiredCity is an Android mobile e-commerce app for Vietnamese traditional fashion targeting Gen Z and Gen Alpha — a generation shaped by cultural pride and a digital-native shopping mindset. The app goes beyond typical e-commerce by layering in AI-powered styling rooted in Vietnamese Five Elements (Ngũ Hành) philosophy.

The standout feature is the Mệnh Calculator: it maps a user's birth year to one of the Five Elements, then recommends products aligned to that element's color palette. An AI Fashion Assistant chatbot — built on n8n and integrated via conversational API — handles styling questions, provides cultural context, and escalates to human support when needed.

What This Project Was About

Existing fashion apps in Vietnam treat clothing as product inventory. TiredCity treats it as culture. The brief was to build an Android e-commerce app that didn't just sell traditional Vietnamese garments — it gave users a reason to care about what they were wearing and how it connected to who they are.

The differentiator: AI rooted in Ngũ Hành

The Mệnh Calculator takes a user's birth year, maps it to one of the Five Elements (Kim · Mộc · Thủy · Hỏa · Thổ), and recommends products whose color palette aligns with that element. It's cultural astrology, personalization, and product discovery in one feature.

The AI Fashion Assistant chatbot — powered by n8n with Gemini 2.5 Flash — handles the questions the Mệnh Calculator can't: "does this áo dài suit my body type?", "what accessories go with this?", "what's the difference between Việt Phục and regular áo dài?" System prompt engineering and graceful degradation to human support were documented as part of the AI integration spec.

Understanding the Market Gap

The problem with existing options isn't that no one sells traditional Vietnamese fashion online — it's that the experience is generic. Grab any mainstream fashion app, swap the product catalog, and nothing really changes. TiredCity was designed to be different from the ground up.

  • Target segment — Gen Z and Gen Alpha: Vietnamese youth who grew up with smartphones but are increasingly reconnecting with cultural identity through fashion, music, and content
  • Pain points identified — no personalization layer, no cultural storytelling, no styling guidance beyond basic sizing charts, no way to connect product choices to individual identity
  • Competitor analysis — mainstream fashion apps (Shopee, Lazada, local boutique apps) all treat traditional fashion as a subcategory rather than the core experience
The insight that shaped the design

Gen Z in Vietnam is drawn to Ngũ Hành aesthetics — Five Elements color palettes are trending on TikTok. Building the Mệnh Calculator wasn't just a novelty feature; it tapped directly into how this generation already thinks about style and identity.

System Design

The system design covered both the customer-facing app and a separate admin app, with full documentation for handoff to the dev team.

Diagrams produced:

  • Sitemap — full navigation structure for both customer and admin flows
  • Use case diagrams — four actors: Guest, Customer Shopping, Customer AI & Profile, Admin
  • Data Flow Diagrams — context diagram (Level 0) and Level 1 breakdowns for each major module
  • Activity diagrams — sign-up/login, add to cart, checkout, AI Mệnh Styling, order management, account management
  • MongoDB schema — database design covering users, products, orders, wishlist, chatbot sessions, and admin management
AI integration spec

The n8n chatbot integration included system prompt engineering to keep responses culturally accurate, graceful degradation (chatbot signals when a question exceeds its scope and escalates to human support), and documentation of the conversational API handshake between the Android client and the n8n workflow engine.

Key screens documented: onboarding + auth (Customer/Admin role split), personalized home, category + search, product detail, AI Mệnh Styling screen with element color mapping, AI Fashion Assistant chat, Store Directory, Articles (Nhật Báo), Events, cart, checkout, and profile. Admin app: dashboard analytics, account/product/order/voucher management.

What the Team Delivered

  • Android customer app — full shopping experience from onboarding through checkout, with personalized home screen and profile management
  • AI Mệnh Styling feature — birth year → Five Elements mapping → color-aligned product recommendations
  • AI Fashion Assistant chatbot — n8n workflow integrated via conversational API, with Gemini 2.5 Flash as the reasoning engine
  • Admin Android app — separate app for dashboard analytics, account management, product catalog, order processing, and voucher management
  • Full system documentation — sitemap, use case diagrams, DFDs, activity diagrams, MongoDB schema
  • High-fidelity Figma screens — wireframes and final designs for all customer and admin flows

My Contribution

The pieces I was most involved in:

  • System analysis — use case diagrams, DFDs, and activity diagrams for key user flows
  • UI/UX design — wireframes and high-fidelity screens for the customer app, including the AI Mệnh Styling screen and AI Fashion Assistant chat interface
  • Admin interface — design and development work on the admin panel's core modules
  • AI integration documentation — system prompt spec and graceful degradation logic for the n8n chatbot

What I Took Away

"Designing for culture is different from designing for function. You have to understand what something means before you can make it useful."

The Mệnh Calculator was the most interesting design challenge I've worked on. It's not a standard recommendation engine — it's a feature that only makes sense if you understand how Ngũ Hành actually works and why a Vietnamese Gen Z user would care about it. Getting that right required research that went way beyond normal product discovery.

The AI chatbot integration also taught me something I didn't expect: the hardest part of AI features isn't the AI — it's defining the boundaries. Knowing when the chatbot should answer versus when it should escalate is a product decision that requires real thought about user trust, accuracy risk, and what happens when the model is wrong.
