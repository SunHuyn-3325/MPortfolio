What This Project Was About

Fishwife is a premium American canned seafood brand. The brief was to design and plan a full market entry into Vietnam — not just "which platform to sell on" but the entire retail model: positioning, distribution, operations, content, and KPIs.

The market opportunity

Direct competition in Vietnam's premium imported seafood category is still thin. Using Porter's Five Forces and a Competitive Profile Matrix, we confirmed that Fishwife could carve out a genuine niche — but only if it led with brand story and product quality rather than competing on price.

Target customer: Gen Z and Millennials in major Vietnamese cities (Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City) who prioritize health-conscious, aesthetically driven, and sustainable consumption. A customer persona and journey map documented how this segment discovers, evaluates, and purchases products online.

Research & Analysis

  • Porter's Five Forces — mapped competitive intensity, supplier power, buyer power, substitution risk, and new entrant threat for premium imported canned seafood in Vietnam
  • Competitive Profile Matrix (CPM) — scored Fishwife against comparable brands on key success factors including pricing, packaging, digital presence, and logistics
  • Customer personas — built two primary personas representing health-conscious Gen Z and sustainability-minded Millennial buyers
  • Customer Journey Map — traced the full path from awareness (social discovery) through consideration, purchase, and post-purchase sharing
  • Business Model Canvas — documented Fishwife's value proposition, key activities, cost structure, and revenue streams for the Vietnam market
Key finding

No strong local competitor exists yet in the premium imported canned seafood space. The window is open — but brand storytelling (sustainability, craft, American heritage) matters more than price at this tier.

Omnichannel Strategy

The distribution plan covered 6 channels simultaneously, each with a distinct role:

  • Shopee & Lazada — primary transaction platforms for reach and volume, with promotions aligned to platform campaigns (Flash Sale, Super Brand Day)
  • TikTok Shop — content-led commerce, product storytelling through short-form video and livestream
  • Facebook & Instagram — brand building and community, lifestyle content targeting the health-conscious segment
  • Haravan website — owned channel for higher-margin direct sales, Core Web Vitals optimization for page speed and SEO
Content strategy: AISAS + 3C framework

Content was mapped to five buyer stages — Attention, Interest, Search, Action, Share — with the 3C framework (Content · Community · Commerce) guiding what type of content sits at each stage and which channel delivers it.

Back-end operations covered: product catalog management, real-time inventory synchronization across platforms, order fulfillment workflow, and payment processing for both domestic and imported product lines.

What We Delivered

  • Full retail business plan — strategic objectives, Business Model Canvas, and risk management framework for Vietnam market entry
  • Omnichannel distribution plan — 6-channel strategy with platform-specific roles, promotional calendars, and KPI targets for each channel
  • Website UX & structure — information architecture, Core Web Vitals targets, and UX recommendations for the Haravan-hosted storefront
  • Content strategy — AISAS-mapped content plan with the 3C (Content–Community–Commerce) framework applied per channel
  • Operations playbook — back-end workflow covering inventory sync, order fulfillment, and payment processing
  • KPI framework — metrics defined for each platform to measure campaign effectiveness and platform-level performance

My Contribution

  • Competitor research and market mapping (Porter's Five Forces, CPM)
  • Customer persona development and journey mapping
  • Content strategy — AISAS framework application per channel
  • Platform strategy for TikTok Shop and the Haravan website
  • Presentation structure and visual storytelling for the final deck

What I Took Away

"Selling on six platforms sounds impressive. Actually coordinating them without overlap or contradiction is the real work."

The biggest lesson: an omnichannel strategy only works when each channel has a defined role — otherwise you're just duplicating effort and competing with yourself on price. Getting clarity on what each platform is for (reach vs. brand vs. direct sales) made every subsequent decision easier.

I also came away with a much clearer sense of how to use frameworks like Porter's Five Forces as actual thinking tools rather than checkboxes. Filling in the matrix is easy — the value comes from knowing which forces matter most for your specific product and market.
